# Semiclassical wave propagation beyond the Ehrenfest time on hyperbolic surfaces

This repository contains the manuscript of that title and its Lean 4 formalization.

The manuscript proves that the semiclassical approximations of Lagrangian states on hyperbolic surfaces
introduced in

> R. Schubert, *Semiclassical wave propagation for large times*, arXiv:0705.0134v1 (2007),
> withdrawn by its author in v2 (2011) for a gap in the proof of the dispersive estimates,

are accurate up to times of order ħ^(-1/2) for fixed analytic radius, with explicit factorial dependence on the expansion and derivative orders. The strategy is
Schubert's. The principal theorem covers every bounded range of analytic radii and uses Schubert’s original amplitude norm; companion results treat the reversed order of weights. The two main corrections to the preprint are: the error of the leading
approximation is of order ħt, and on surfaces with cusps the pointwise bound carries a factor that grows with
the cusp height. Schubert's files are not part of this repository.

## Contents

- `paper/`: LaTeX source of the manuscript (`paper.tex` with its input files). The ten main sections develop the
  quotient remainder, analytic estimates, approximation proofs, consequences, sharpness and escape of mass.
  The approximation proofs proceed from the power weight to the reversed weight order and then the original norm,
  with the critical-weight estimates placed beside the original-norm argument. Cusp-factor sharpness has its own
  section; the modular-surface application is stated in the introduction and proved through named intermediate lemmas.
  Three appendices contain auxiliary special-function/kernel calculations, formalization documentation, and a detailed comparison with Schubert's preprint.
  Build from `paper/` with `pdflatex paper.tex`
  (run it three times).
- `lean/`: the Lean 4 development, namespace `Qschubert` (Lean `v4.35.0`, Mathlib `v4.35.0`, `physicslib4`, and
  `PrimeNumberTheoremAnd` for the zero-free region of the Riemann zeta function).
  Appendix "Formalization" of the manuscript says which statements are formalized, in which reading, and
  which are not.
- `LEAN_STATUS.md`: for every labelled statement of the manuscript, whether it is formalized and by which Lean
  theorems. Generated by `paper/lean_status.py` from `paper/lean_status_rows.tex`.
- `.github/workflows/lean.yml`: builds `lean/` from a fresh checkout and runs the two axiom audits. The build
  runs when `lean/` has changed; a commit that leaves `lean/` unchanged takes over the result of the last run
  on the same Lean sources.

## Checking the formalization

```
cd lean
lake exe cache get
lake build
lake env lean scripts/Axioms.lean    > axioms.log
lake env lean scripts/AxiomsAll.lean > axioms_all.log
python3 -u scripts/check_axioms.py named axioms.log scripts/Axioms.lean
python3 -u scripts/check_axioms.py all axioms_all.log
```

`scripts/Axioms.lean` prints the axioms of the named declarations; `scripts/AxiomsAll.lean` checks every
declaration of the project. Both must report only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

Comments in the Lean sources and in the LaTeX sources refer in places to working documents of the project
(`lean/BLUEPRINT.md`, files under `notes/`, review numbers such as `R60`) and to line numbers of the LaTeX
source of arXiv:0705.0134v1. These working documents are not part of this repository; nothing in the
manuscript or in the formal statements depends on them.

A kernel check certifies the proofs of the formal statements. Whether a formal statement says what the
corresponding statement of the manuscript says is a matter of reading; the manuscript's appendix lists the
reading choices.
